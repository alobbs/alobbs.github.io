Breaking the 1.58-bit Barrier for Ternary LLMs

Researchers introduce BITCOS, a distribution-adaptive storage layout for ternary LLMs that uses a presence bitmap plus compacted sign vector, costing 2−z bits per weight based on zero density z. After measuring 29 ternary models, they find zeros make up as much as 51.5% of weights, letting BITCOS outperform conventional five-trit packing in 26 models and reach 1.485 bits per weight on the sparsest. With optimized CPU/GPU unpacking, BITCOS improves ternary matrix-vector kernels by up to 1.28× and end-to-end decode throughput by up to 1.18× on CPUs and 1.27× on GPUs.
