Are Latent Reasoning Models Easily Interpretable?

Latent reasoning models often bypass their hidden reasoning tokens on logical tasks, producing the same answers even when forced to stop early, suggesting their performance stems largely from training data rather than latent inference. However, when reasoning tokens are needed for math problems, researchers decoded correct intermediate steps from hidden states up to 93% of the time, showing these models usually implement interpretable, standard solutions. This interpretability can also serve as a signal for whether a model’s prediction is likely correct.

AI Isn’t Outthinking Mathematicians. It’s Out-Remembering Them

AI’s mathematical performance may stem less from superior reasoning and more from its access to a vast external symbolic working memory, which removes the severe capacity limits of human working memory. Human mathematics is constrained by the brain’s ability to hold and manipulate only small amounts of information at once, whereas AI can keep entire problems, equations, and approaches in its context window. Thus, AI’s advantage may be expanded working memory rather than fundamentally better thinking.

The essay defines power as responsibility: having power over someone means being responsible for them, and even physical coercion can be understood through the responsibilities involved in using force. It argues that power is never absolute and can be delegated, with delegation itself generating new responsibilities. The discussion then examines this recursive structure of delegated power using mathematical category theory.

Semaglutide linked to 26% lower 5-year predicted dementia risk

Compared with placebo, semaglutide reduced the increase in predicted 5-year dementia risk by 26% and lowered the odds of a higher dementia risk classification by 36%.

Researchers recommend prioritizing open-source software and other measures to make these technologies more accountable.

Could a computer scientist build a brain?

A computer scientist tasked with programming a single cell to build a brain faces hard algorithmic constraints: the genome is too small for per-synapse wiring, and blind axon search would take too long. These scaling limits drive solutions that resemble real developmental biology, suggesting some neural development features are computational necessities rather than historical accidents. This perspective could also inspire new approaches in machine learning.

388 years ago, Galileo worked out why human giants can’t exist

Galileo Galilei argued that humanlike giants are physically impossible, using a beam analogy to show that strength scales with cross-sectional area (the square of size) while weight scales with volume (the cube of size). Therefore, a proportionally larger giant’s bones would be too weak to support their own weight.

The End of Mathematics

The article presents a cautionary scenario, not a prediction, where AI generates a vast surplus of mathematical results while human mathematical communities atrophy, leading to duplicative, low-value work. Existing incentives reward producing papers and solving conjectures, but these outputs become mispriced, pushing mathematicians toward AI-assisted “conjecture slot machines” with little genuine engagement. The author emphasizes this is a warning about current trends, not a forecast.
