TLA+ Verification: Capabilities and Limitations

Recent claims that Claude Opus used TLA+ to find race conditions have fueled hype that formal verification can solve agentic software development, but a longtime TLA+ advocate cautions this is overblown. TLA+ is strong for modeling concurrent systems and checking expressible temporal properties such as invariants, safety, and liveness, but it can only verify properties that can be formally specified, and some desirable properties cannot be expressed in TLA+ at all. Verification also requires a property to verify, and a correct TLA+ design does not automatically guarantee correct code.

SDF vs. MSDF vs. Slug: GPU Text Rendering

GPU text rendering is challenging because glyphs are vector outlines that must be filled and antialiased crisply at arbitrary scales and transforms. Texture atlases bake glyphs into bitmaps but degrade when scaled and become memory-heavy for large scripts, while SDF/MSDF and Slug offer scalable alternatives. Slug, patented by Eric Lengyel in 2019 and dedicated to the public domain in 2026, renders glyphs directly from outlines in the fragment shader without an atlas or per-frame tessellation.
