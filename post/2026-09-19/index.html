How Hacker News ranking works: scoring, controversy, and penalties (2013)

Hacker News ranks stories using a formula based on upvotes, time since submission, and penalties, with a “gravity” exponent causing scores to decay so nothing stays on the front page too long. For efficiency, stories are reranked only when upvoted, while one of the top 50 is randomly reranked every 30 seconds and pages can be cached for 90 seconds. Controversy and other penalties can sharply lower or remove otherwise high-scoring articles from the rankings.

Roman Timekeeping

Roman timekeeping divided days according to available technology, initially into ante meridiem and post meridiem and later, after the Greek sundial reached Rome around 293 BC, into twelve seasonal hours from sunrise to sunset. These hours varied in length by season and latitude, while the civil day ran from midnight to midnight and the natural day from sunrise to sunset. Romans used sundials and water clocks, and their dawn-based day influenced terms such as siesta and Catholic canonical hours.
