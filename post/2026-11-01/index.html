Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs

A new paper proves a Platonic Representation Theorem showing that Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) trained separately on multiple modalities learn the same representation up to one rotation when given good views, and frames next-token prediction as a JEPA with bad views that aligns only through scale. Using 145k captions and texts from 940M web images, the authors validate the theory, derive a scaling law of alignment, and find good views need 42 times fewer training samples than next-token prediction. They report that a 109M-parameter text JEPA aligns with DINOv2 and CLIP image encoders better than evaluated language models up to 72B parameters, concluding that positive views, not scale alone, are the key to platonic representations.

Leaking Secrets from Air-Gap Computers by Spelling Radio Signals from RAM

Researchers present RAMBO, an attack in which malware on a compromised air-gapped computer encodes sensitive information into software-generated radio signals from RAM that can be intercepted with SDR hardware and an off-the-shelf antenna and decoded. The method can leak data such as files, images, keylogs, biometric information, and encryption keys at up to 1,000 bits per second, and the paper also proposes countermeasures.

Non-Determinism and Overcount on Modern Hardware Performance Counter Implementations

A study of eleven x86_64 CPU implementations finds that hardware performance counters expected to be exact, such as retired instructions, show run-to-run variation and overcounting, partly because the iret interrupt-return instruction is counted as a userspace instruction. Only a few events, such as conditional branches and retired stores, are deterministic enough for strict deterministic execution environments. ARM, IA64, POWER, and SPARC counters appear more deterministic, while workarounds for x86_64 are often impossible without PMU redesign.

  • Non-Determinism and Overcount on Modern Hardware Performance Counter Implementations — web.eece.maine.edu

Apollo 14 astronaut Stuart Roosa carried hundreds of tree seeds in his personal kit while orbiting the Moon in 1971. After returning to Earth, the U.S. Forest Service germinated the seeds despite an accidental exposure to vacuum, producing “Moon Trees” that were planted across the United States and worldwide, often for the 1976 bicentennial. The trees serve as a living tribute to Roosa and the Apollo program.
