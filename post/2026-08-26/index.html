Agentic Context Management: Memory and Cost as Architecture Problems

Agentic Context Management (ACM) reframes AI agent memory failures as a lifecycle and architecture problem rather than a storage-and-retrieval issue, defining five primitives: architecting, ingesting, scoping, anticipating, and compacting & consolidation. It argues that naive context accumulation incurs quadratic token costs, while validated compaction achieves linear cost with preserved accuracy. A reference implementation, Maximem Synap, reports 92% on LongMemEval and 93.2% on LoCoMo.

mold: A Massively Parallel Linker

mold is a Unix/Linux linker that applies data parallelism across the entire linking pipeline, decoupling symbol resolution and archive processing to overcome scalability limits of existing linkers. It links multi-gigabyte debug binaries in at most a few seconds, often under one second, making it 2.4–16.1x faster than lld and up to 112x faster than GNU ld. The speedup comes from the cumulative effect of parallelizing all passes rather than any single optimization.

  • mold: A Massively Parallel Linker — arxiv.org

Harvest (IBM 7950): Supercomputer for cryptanalysis at the NSA in the Cold War

During the Cold War, IBM built the Harvest computer for the NSA, a secret cryptanalytic processor that operated from 1962 to 1975 and ran jobs up to 200 times faster than other computers of its era. It pioneered streaming data processing, an automated tape library, and the custom programming language Alpha, foreshadowing modern specialized computing. Its classified history illustrates how national security demands drove computing technology far ahead of civilian development.
