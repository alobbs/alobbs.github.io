Cherenkov Radiation - traveling faster than light

Cherenkov radiation is the blue or violet glow produced when charged particles travel faster than light in a medium, a discovery by Pavel Cherenkov recognized with the 1958 Nobel Prize in Physics. It occurs when displaced atoms emit high-frequency photons, similar to a sonic boom on the light spectrum. The IAEA uses Cherenkov viewing devices at nuclear facilities, including spent fuel ponds, to verify declared nuclear material and help prevent diversion from peaceful use.

  • Cherenkov Radiation - traveling faster than light — iaea.org

Neijuan

Neijuan, or involution, is a Chinese term describing intensified effort, complexity, or competition without corresponding gains in output, efficiency, or collective benefit; it is used to analyze resource inequality, economic malaise, higher education, and labor markets, often leading to overproduction, diminishing returns, and personal overwork, stress, and entrapment. The concept originated in anthropology through Alexander Goldenweiser and Clifford Geertz and later entered Chinese rural studies via Prasenjit Duara and Philip Huang.
