## 🤖 AI & Machine Learning

### Energy-Based Reasoners: 385x more efficient inference

Researchers introduce Energy-Based Reasoners (EBRs), which learn an energy function over latent reasoning states and use gradient descent to score, refine, stop, and select candidate predictions without an external verifier. With 1.8M parameters, EBRs report 99.9% accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme using 345× less inference compute than the next-best model (the headline cites 385×), and they achieve state-of-the-art coverage with near-100% single-sample accuracy on N-Queens and Graph Coloring.

- [Energy-Based Reasoners: 385x more efficient inference](https://energy-based-reasoners.github.io/) — *energy-based-reasoners.github.io*

### Why is Gemini 4 Carbon taking so long?

Gemini 4 Carbon is substantially slower than Opus and Astra in real-world coding tasks. Analysis found slower inference explains only part of the gap, while most delay comes from longer trajectories—about seven times as many steps as Astra and eleven times as many actions—with half its steps spent on unrequested work such as auditing code beyond the bug. On low effort, Carbon often fails to produce a fix, instead going in circles.

- [Why is Gemini 4 Carbon taking so long?](https://kernel.dev/blog/why-is-gemini-4-carbon-taking-so-long/) — *kernel.dev*

### Claude was trained on protein sequences at scale

A next-amino-acid test on little-studied bacterial and archaeal proteins found that the latest Claude models were trained on protein sequences at scale. Claude Fable 5.2 predicted the next amino acid correctly 54% of the time on real proteins versus 9% on shuffled versions, while other frontier and open-weight models stayed near zero. The models also did better on larger protein families, consistent with learning from sequences.

- [Claude was trained on protein sequences at scale](http://xinmingtu.cn/blog/2026/claude-opus-5-5-protein-sequences/) — *xinmingtu.cn*

### Why Fibonacci?

The article explains the design principles behind Fibonacci LED boards, which place LEDs on a Fermat spiral using the golden angle of about 137.508° and √n spacing. This arrangement avoids spokes and keeps pixel density even across the board. The author credits Jim Bumgardner and Nick Pisarro as inspirations for exploring and explaining the technique.

### RBRD: RAM-Backed Linux Block Driver Written In Rust For Better Safety But Slower Performance

Linux kernel patches introduce RBRD, a RAM-backed block driver written in Rust as a safer alternative to the C-based BRD driver. RBRD avoids simultaneous read/write page copies to prevent undefined behavior, but it is slower: writes incur about a 19% single-thread and 22% eight-thread performance hit, while reads are 2–10% slower. Developers argue the page-integrity and safety benefits justify the performance cost.

- [RBRD: RAM-Backed Linux Block Driver Written In Rust For Better Safety But Slower Performance](https://www.phoronix.com/news/RBRD-Rust-Linux-RAM-Driver) — *phoronix.com*
