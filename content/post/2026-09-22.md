### GPT-6 Astra Cracks Enigma Message

OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra independently broke the long-resistant German Army Enigma message MVUEH (Nr. 172) from 10 July 1941, developing its own Enigma simulator and Bombe software and using a repeated “ROSENOW” crib to recover a key with wheel order 253. The plaintext closely matched the previously broken message SIPVX, though the break was complicated by transcription errors and an unusual left-hand wheel turnover at the 72nd letter. The effort followed Carter Leffer’s request to test Crypto Cellar Research’s unbroken messages, and researchers are still analyzing Astra’s logs, including how it obtained file references and whether it accessed Bundesarchiv digitized collections or a private collection.

### The Economics of Open-Weight Inference

The paper argues that open-weight inference demand can extend the useful earning life of older NVIDIA GPUs, challenging the assumption that each new generation makes prior ones obsolete. It finds open-weight models can be cheaper than closed models at comparable intelligence, with self-hosting on rented hardware costing $0.12–$0.35 per million output tokens at full utilization, and the A100 sometimes producing output more cheaply than the H100 on gpt-oss-120b. Rental data show the five-year A100 price retaining 80.2% of its one-month price versus 43.7–59.8% for Hopper and 53.8% for Blackwell, though the paper does not establish that open-weight demand caused A100 occupancy or pricing behavior.

- [The Economics of Open-Weight Inference](https://data.ornn.com/publications/the-economics-of-open-weight-inference) — *data.ornn.com*

Unhealthy food and drink brands dominated detected branding at 70.4%, followed by prediction markets, alcohol, gambling, and cryptocurrency, with four major brands accounting for 65% of all harmful logos. This embedded marketing generated an estimated 267 billion impressions in the UK alone, making exposure unavoidable for viewers.

### The secret Cold War bioweapons test that exposed millions in the Bay Area

In 1950, the U.S. Army secretly sprayed San Francisco with Serratia marcescens bacteria from an offshore ship as a Cold War simulation of a biological weapons attack, without informing residents. At least one civilian, 75-year-old Edward Nevin, died after exposure, and his family only learned the truth in 1976 through a newspaper report, sparking public anger and fear over the government’s human experiments.

### What California is learning from solar panels built over irrigation canals

California is testing “solar canals” that place photovoltaic panels over irrigation canals to generate clean electricity while shading the water and reducing evaporation. It aims to address both the state’s clean energy transition and projected water shortages.
