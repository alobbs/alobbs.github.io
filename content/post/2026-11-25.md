### Why Automated Proof-Solving Is the Same Old Dead End

A new paper critiques OpenAI’s October 2026 release of 722 automated mathematical manuscripts, which claimed full or partial solutions to 372 longstanding open problems, through the DeMillo–Lipton–Perlis framework that treats proofs as products of social human critique. It argues that mass-producing dense, unreadable proofs “industrializes noise,” decouples mechanical solving from community vetting, and floods the literature faster than human attention can separate signal from noise. Posting such results, the authors conclude, is not the same as submitting them to the social processes by which mathematics is understood and incorporated.

- [Why Automated Proof-Solving Is the Same Old Dead End](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31286) — *arxiv.org*

### Here in the World: Letters of Albert Camus, 1944–1959

Here in the World collects nearly a thousand intimate letters exchanged by Albert Camus and actress Maria Casarès between 1944 and 1959, edited by Béatrice Vaillant and now translated into English by Sandra Smith and Cory Stockwell.
