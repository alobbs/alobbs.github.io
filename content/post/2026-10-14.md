### AIR-LLM: Broadcasting AI Weights over Radio for Memory-Free Edge LLM Inference via RF Computing

Researchers present AIR-LLM, an architecture that enables memory-free edge LLM inference by broadcasting model weights from a central radio and computing GEMV directly in the RF domain using RF mixers. It uses MIMO spatial multiplexing and a precoder–postcoder pair to reduce airtime and allow one broadcast to serve unlimited users. Evaluated on ray-traced urban channels and real RF mixer profiles, AIR-LLM achieved 4.0% WikiText-2 perplexity degradation on LLaMA-3.1-8B while saving 157.7/40.4 energy and shortening airtime 104.1/26.0 versus FP16 and weight-only quantization baselines.

- [AIR-LLM: Broadcasting AI Weights over Radio for Memory-Free Edge LLM Inference via RF Computing](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00465) — *arxiv.org*

### The Aaronson Oracle

In the early 1950s at Bell Labs, David Hagelbarger built SEER, a relay-based machine that played matching pennies by tracking recent patterns in human opponents to predict their choices. Claude Shannon built a simpler version and calculated that it could be beaten three times out of four in principle, though not by a human in real time. When the two machines played each other, Shannon’s simpler machine defeated Hagelbarger’s.

- [The Aaronson Oracle](https://aaronsonoracle.com/) — *aaronsonoracle.com*

### Lockstep to pipeline in almost one line

The article explains that a simple lockstep tick system can be converted almost trivially into a pipelining tick system. In pipelining, read and write phases run in parallel batches across threads/cores but still synchronize at a barrier each tick, preserving back pressure and overrun tracking. This changes tick time from a summed sequential cost to the maximum of tick, read, and write times, though overlapping read/write batches require transactions.

It says saying “I don’t know” is often smarter than jumping to conclusions, while name-calling only escalates conflict and reveals more about the speaker than the target.

### The road we were told not to take

Marking the 50th anniversary of Amory Lovins’ 1976 Foreign Affairs essay “Energy Strategy: The Road Not Taken?”, the article argues that Lovins’ advocacy for a soft energy path and against nuclear power helped push U.S. policy away from reactors. It claims that a France-style nuclear buildout replacing coal, oil and gas would have avoided 65 billion tons of U.S. CO2 emissions through 2024, or 115 billion tons including other countries—equivalent to 89% of the remaining 1.5°C carbon budget. The piece says Lovins influenced Carter-era policy and has blamed nuclear’s decline on cost, while critics view his stance as central to the climate crisis.

### A guided 3D Leonardo da Vinci museum, built in code

A nonprofit has launched Museum of Ages’ first wing, a free digital Leonardo da Vinci museum featuring a 17-stop virtual tour of his paintings, machines, anatomy sheets, manuscript pages, and reconstructed last house in Amboise. The museum is available in seven languages, runs with WebGPU and a WebGL2 fallback, includes accessibility-focused pre-rendered walkthroughs, and is released under AGPL-3.0 with texts and pictures under CC BY-SA 4.0.

- [A guided 3D Leonardo da Vinci museum, built in code](https://museumofages.org/w/vinci) — *museumofages.org*

### Why is every rationalist blog post way too long?

The article criticizes rationalist bloggers for writing excessively long, tedious posts, arguing that their verbosity is often mistaken for thoroughness and reflects a lack of wisdom or discretion. It cites examples such as Scott Alexander’s 25,000-word post, Eliezer Yudkowsky’s million-word “sequences,” and Gwern’s 9,000-word essay on nicotine to illustrate the tendency.

### Moirélephant

Moirélephant is a web-based exploration of barrier-grid animation inspired by Eadweard Muybridge’s 1887 “Plate Number 733. Elephant walking.” Created for Weird Web October 2025 on the theme of “transparency,” it invites users to drag or scroll to move the piece.

### High-speed video reveals how honey bee stingers pump venom

High-speed video shows that a honey bee’s detached stinger can keep pumping venom after the bee flies away, using a unique double-piston, pump-like mechanism. Researchers filmed stingers inserted into artificial gel and analyzed their movements frame by frame to identify the biomechanics behind this autonomous venom delivery.

- [High-speed video reveals how honey bee stingers pump venom](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/10/honey-bee-stingers-are-double-piston-venom-pumps/) — *arstechnica.com*

### The Feeling of Power

In Isaac Asimov's "The Feeling of Power," programmer Jehan Shuman presents Myron Aub, an aging low-grade technician, to General Weider and Congressman Brant at the New Pentagon. Aub demonstrates the forgotten ability to perform arithmetic by hand, correctly multiplying 9 by 7 and then 17 by 23 on paper with a stylus, astonishing officials who rely on pocket computers. The officials thus witness a rediscovered human skill in a society that has become dependent on machines.
