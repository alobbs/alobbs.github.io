### AIR-LLM: Broadcasting AI Weights over Radio for Memory-Free Edge LLM Inference via RF Computing

Researchers present AIR-LLM, an architecture that enables memory-free edge LLM inference by broadcasting model weights from a central radio and computing GEMV directly in the RF domain using RF mixers. It uses MIMO spatial multiplexing and a precoder–postcoder pair to reduce airtime and allow one broadcast to serve unlimited users. Evaluated on ray-traced urban channels and real RF mixer profiles, AIR-LLM achieved 4.0% WikiText-2 perplexity degradation on LLaMA-3.1-8B while saving 157.7/40.4 energy and shortening airtime 104.1/26.0 versus FP16 and weight-only quantization baselines.

- [AIR-LLM: Broadcasting AI Weights over Radio for Memory-Free Edge LLM Inference via RF Computing](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00465) — *arxiv.org*

### Strands Decider: Why Not an Encoder?

Since launching strands-decider-2B, the author compared modified decoder-LLM designs against encoder models like BERT for classification. Encoder-only models had much lower median latency (~35 ms vs. 65 ms) and similar accuracy, but scaled worse as prompts grew, while decoder layers offered better asymptotic compute costs. The results were not a clear win for encoders, with trade-offs in accuracy, calibration, latency, and generalization.

### The Aaronson Oracle

In the early 1950s at Bell Labs, David Hagelbarger built SEER, a relay-based machine that played matching pennies by tracking recent patterns in human opponents to predict their choices. Claude Shannon built a simpler version and calculated that it could be beaten three times out of four in principle, though not by a human in real time. When the two machines played each other, Shannon’s simpler machine defeated Hagelbarger’s.

- [The Aaronson Oracle](https://aaronsonoracle.com/) — *aaronsonoracle.com*
