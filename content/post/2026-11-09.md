## 🔒 Security & Privacy

### Three suspects arrested for AI-powered impersonation scheme costing victims $129M

US authorities indicted three men for an AI-powered impersonation scheme that used deepfakes of executives and lawyers to trick employees into wiring more than $129 million to accounts in nine countries, where the funds were converted into cryptocurrency. The suspects face wire fraud, aggravated identity theft, and money laundering charges; two were arrested in Spain and one in the UK, with extradition requests pending.

### Pro-Kremlin group leaks EU officials’ private data, offers bounties for more info on family members

Pro-Kremlin group NoName057(16) published personal data on 18 EU politicians, law enforcement officials, and journalists it calls hostile to Moscow, and offered cryptocurrency bounties for more sensitive information about them and their families. The doxxing appears to retaliate for Operation Eastwood, an international law enforcement effort that disrupted the group’s botnet and took over 100 servers offline. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani, whose data was exposed, said he would not be intimidated.

### Linux 7.5 To Convey To User-Space Multi-Grain Timestamp Usage On Files

Linux 7.5 will add a new statx attribute, STATX_ATTR_MULTIGRAIN_TIMESTAMP, so user-space can tell whether a file uses multigrain timestamps. Multigrain timestamp support, merged in Linux 6.13, allows switching between coarse and fine-grained file timestamp reporting and was originally aimed at NFS caching issues. The patch by Jeff Layton is in the VFS.git tree and is expected to be submitted for the Linux 7.5 merge window.

- [Linux 7.5 To Convey To User-Space Multi-Grain Timestamp Usage On Files](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linux-7.5-Statx-MG-TImestamp) — *phoronix.com*

### A C++ Quicksort Faster Than std::sort

blqsort is a very fast general-purpose comparison-based quicksort using branchless partitioning, sorting networks for small partitions, and robust pivot selection. Benchmarks on 50 million random doubles and structs show it outperforming std::sort and an ipnsort C++ port on Apple M1 and Intel i5 systems, with multithreaded C versions roughly 3× faster on M1. It is available on GitHub under the MIT license as C++ and single-header C versions, including multithreaded variants, and efficiently handles sorted, reverse-sorted, and duplicate-heavy inputs.
