### Breaking the 1.58-bit Barrier for Ternary LLMs

Researchers introduce BITCOS, a distribution-adaptive storage layout for ternary LLMs that uses a presence bitmap plus compacted sign vector, costing 2−z bits per weight based on zero density z. After measuring 29 ternary models, they find zeros make up as much as 51.5% of weights, letting BITCOS outperform conventional five-trit packing in 26 models and reach 1.485 bits per weight on the sparsest. With optimized CPU/GPU unpacking, BITCOS improves ternary matrix-vector kernels by up to 1.28× and end-to-end decode throughput by up to 1.18× on CPUs and 1.27× on GPUs.

- [Breaking the 1.58-bit Barrier for Ternary LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16338) — *arxiv.org*

### How good are frontier models at physics?

A new expert re-grading of six widely used physics benchmarks finds that most model responses initially marked incorrect were due to flawed evaluations, erroneous reference solutions, or ambiguous questions rather than genuine physics reasoning errors. After corrections, frontier model GPT-5.6-Sol’s scores rose substantially—for example, mean@4 from 47.3% to 78.7% on HLE-Physics and from 61.0% to 87.2% on CMT-Benchmark, with corrected pass@4 of 94.4% on retained CritPt challenges. The authors conclude that current benchmarks understate frontier models’ physics abilities and are nearing saturation, highlighting the need for more demanding, expert-validated evaluations.

- [How good are frontier models at physics?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13009) — *arxiv.org*
