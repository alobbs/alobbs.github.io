## 🤖 AI Policy & Society

### OpenAI drops Caltech math hackathon sponsorship after 'slop mathematics' criticism

OpenAI said it is no longer sponsoring Caltech’s math hackathon after current and former Caltech mathematicians criticized the AI-sponsored event as potentially destructive and accused AI companies of producing “slop mathematics”—claiming results while leaving verification to unpaid researchers. The withdrawal followed broader controversy over OpenAI’s claim to have solved the Navier-Stokes problem and whether it used a mathematician’s chatbot conversations. Event organizers said they did not intend to cause such a stir, while Anthropic remains a sponsor.

- [OpenAI drops Caltech math hackathon sponsorship after 'slop mathematics' criticism](https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-caltech-ai-math-hackathon-backlash-anthropic-2026-9) — *businessinsider.com*

### Garry Tan: 'I'd do nothing' on China AI distillation; urges current AI risks, not extinction fears

Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan said he would take no action on Chinese AI model distillation and suggested the U.S. could even consider its own distillation regime. He urged regulators to balance open-weight and frontier models and focus AI safety on near-term, science-based risks like cybersecurity and bioweapons rather than doomsday extinction fears. At YC’s Demo Day, 149 of 196 presenting startups were machine-learning and AI ventures.

- [Garry Tan: 'I'd do nothing' on China AI distillation; urges current AI risks, not extinction fears](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/y-combinator-garry-tan-says-do-nothing-about-distillation.html) — *cnbc.com*

## 🔐 Security & Privacy

### US court sentences Ukrainian Oleksii Lytvynenko to 4 years for Conti ransomware wire fraud

A U.S. court sentenced Ukrainian national Oleksii Lytvynenko to four years in prison for conspiracy to commit wire fraud connected to Conti ransomware attacks between 2020 and 2022. Lytvynenko, 44, was arrested in Ireland in July 2023 and extradited to the U.S.; he admitted to controlling stolen data from 12 victims and helping code malware used by the gang. The DOJ said Conti attacks affected 47 states, 31 foreign countries, and caused over $150 million in victim payouts as of January 2022.

- [US court sentences Ukrainian Oleksii Lytvynenko to 4 years for Conti ransomware wire fraud](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/conti-ransomware-gang-member-sentenced-to-four-years-in-prison/) — *bleepingcomputer.com*

### US judge dismisses LinkedIn browser-extension scanning lawsuits, says users voluntarily expose data

A US judge dismissed two lawsuits accusing LinkedIn of scanning users’ browser extensions, ruling the plaintiffs lacked standing because they did not show they had extensions that conveyed private information to LinkedIn. The judge allowed leave to amend but said a privacy violation claim was unlikely because users voluntarily download extensions that intentionally expose data to websites. One plaintiff’s lawyer said he may pursue a state-court claim or appeal to the Ninth Circuit.

- [US judge dismisses LinkedIn browser-extension scanning lawsuits, says users voluntarily expose data](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/linkedin-beats-browsergate-lawsuits-over-scanning-users-chrome-extensions/) — *arstechnica.com*

### Hundreds of AI-powered attacks exploit PaperCut flaws

PaperCut released a maintenance update replacing earlier emergency patches for two serious vulnerabilities. GreyNoise reported that AI-assisted attackers exploited the flaws to compromise at least 395 organizations in 48 countries, mostly in the U.S. education sector, while avoiding targets in Russia, China, Iran, and other countries. Researchers said the campaign shows how AI tools let attackers move faster and scale with minimal human input.

- [Hundreds of AI-powered attacks exploit PaperCut flaws](https://cybernews.com/security/papercut-vulnerabilities-ai-powered-attacks/) — *cybernews.com*

A U.S. District Judge in Texas found TikTok liable for misleading users about its safety standards, including claims that it removed inappropriate and explicit content accessible to minors. A trial is expected next month to determine penalties and relief.
