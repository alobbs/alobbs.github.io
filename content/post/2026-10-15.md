## 🤖 AI & Machine Learning

### Speculative Decoding: The Art of Guessing Well

Speculative decoding reduces LLM inference latency by using a smaller model to guess several tokens ahead while the larger target model verifies them in parallel, without changing the final sampling distribution. The article derives the method from first principles and surveys modern drafters such as EAGLE-3, multi-token prediction, and block-diffusion systems like DFlash and DSpark. It also discusses how to train drafters and why minimizing KL divergence is not quite the desired objective.

- [Speculative Decoding: The Art of Guessing Well](https://astralord.github.io/posts/speculative-decoding-the-art-of-guessing-well/) — *astralord.github.io*

### Build your own decision model

Nish Tahir demonstrates how to turn a small Qwen model into a fixed-choice decision model by masking outputs to allowed answer tokens, enabling single-pass selection among options A–E. He evaluates and fine-tunes it on CommonsenseQA, then applies temperature scaling because the raw token probabilities are overconfident and need to better match actual accuracy.

- [Build your own decision model](https://nishtahir.com/build-your-own-decision-model/) — *nishtahir.com*

### Neural Networks More Interpretable Than Believed

Recent articles argue that neural networks are more interpretable than previously believed, with most neurons understandable even at trillion-parameter scales. They claim the canonical evidence for polysemanticity and superposition was misread, and that apparent feature mixing in models like InceptionV1 came largely from architectural issues and weak tools, or from recursive generalization, rather than inherent superposition. As architectural improvements make later layers more legible, mechanistic interpretability can study networks directly instead of first untangling them.
