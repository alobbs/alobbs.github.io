### Are Latent Reasoning Models Easily Interpretable?

Latent reasoning models often bypass their hidden reasoning tokens on logical tasks, producing the same answers even when forced to stop early, suggesting their performance stems largely from training data rather than latent inference. However, when reasoning tokens are needed for math problems, researchers decoded correct intermediate steps from hidden states up to 93% of the time, showing these models usually implement interpretable, standard solutions. This interpretability can also serve as a signal for whether a model’s prediction is likely correct.

- [Are Latent Reasoning Models Easily Interpretable?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04902) — *arxiv.org*

### AI Isn't Outthinking Mathematicians. It's Out-Remembering Them

AI’s mathematical performance may stem less from superior reasoning and more from its access to a vast external symbolic working memory, which removes the severe capacity limits of human working memory. Human mathematics is constrained by the brain’s ability to hold and manipulate only small amounts of information at once, whereas AI can keep entire problems, equations, and approaches in its context window. Thus, AI’s advantage may be expanded working memory rather than fundamentally better thinking.
