## 🔒 Security & Privacy

### Brazilian Court Sanctions Lawyers for Prompt Injection in Court Filing

A Brazilian labor court censured and fined two lawyers after they hid a prompt-injection instruction in a court filing intended to manipulate the AI system reviewing it. The AI tool detected the white-on-white text and blocked it, and the court imposed a fine of about $16,000, in what may be the first judicial ruling globally addressing intentional manipulation of a court’s AI system.

- [Brazilian Court Sanctions Lawyers for Prompt Injection in Court Filing](https://jetstream.security/insights/brazilian-court-sanctions-prompt-injection-filing/) — *jetstream.security*

### Leaking Secrets from Air-Gap Computers by Spelling Radio Signals from RAM

Researchers present RAMBO, an attack in which malware on a compromised air-gapped computer encodes sensitive information into software-generated radio signals from RAM that can be intercepted with SDR hardware and an off-the-shelf antenna and decoded. The method can leak data such as files, images, keylogs, biometric information, and encryption keys at up to 1,000 bits per second, and the paper also proposes countermeasures.

- [Leaking Secrets from Air-Gap Computers by Spelling Radio Signals from RAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02292) — *arxiv.org*

### OTape

oTAPE 1.0 is a release candidate for an open specification defining .otape, a self-contained offline file format for digital cassettes. Each .otape file is a ZIP+JSON archive holding both fixed-length sides as continuous recordings plus the label, J-card, and record tabs. A browser-based Web Deck plays .otape files locally and can emulate vintage tape-machine sound without altering the file.

### Non-Determinism and Overcount on Modern Hardware Performance Counter Implementations

A study of eleven x86_64 CPU implementations finds that hardware performance counters expected to be exact, such as retired instructions, show run-to-run variation and overcounting, partly because the iret interrupt-return instruction is counted as a userspace instruction. Only a few events, such as conditional branches and retired stores, are deterministic enough for strict deterministic execution environments. ARM, IA64, POWER, and SPARC counters appear more deterministic, while workarounds for x86_64 are often impossible without PMU redesign.

- [Non-Determinism and Overcount on Modern Hardware Performance Counter Implementations](https://web.eece.maine.edu/~vweaver/projects/deterministic/deterministic_counters.pdf) — *web.eece.maine.edu*
